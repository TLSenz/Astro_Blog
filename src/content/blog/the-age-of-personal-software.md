With the advance of LLMs and the rise of vibe coding, the difficulty of creating software has gone down rapidly. It is now trivial to open Codex or OpenCode and create your own app or your own website. You're a prompt away from your personal software. I personally think this is great. I can execute any idea and build an MVP or even a fully functional application I can use in my day-to-day life. Recently I had an issue. I am currently learning Japanese, and one way to learn it is to listen to Japanese podcasts and conversations. So I prompted Claude to build me an Android app that searches for Japanese podcasts and conversations on YouTube, with the ability to add podcasts myself if I find one I love. And so 10 minutes later I had a functioning app installed on my Android smartphone, and I must say, the app worked. It was not perfect, but the app made it easier to listen to Japanese. Now, I am a software developer, at least I think of myself as one. But many people don't know about the possibility of creating your own software. Imagine the potentially great ideas that exist in the minds of normal people. I have already solved many problems in my own life.

## The Advantages of Personal Software

Personal software has many advantages. As the name states, it can be personalized and adapted to the specific use case and to the life of a person in ways which commercial software cannot. For example, I organise a sports tournament for an event. Now, with LLMs and agents, I can easily create software which sorts the people that play in the tournament into teams, creates a game plan and provides a leaderboard. It lowers the entry into software to one prompt.

## Security Issues

Like in everything, there are also problems. Security is a big one, but I think the better models get, the smaller the problem will be. And also, with smaller applications that don't hold critical data, it is not the end of the world. Don't get me wrong, applications should be secure, but nobody cares if your tournament app gets hacked. Implementing security should be a default point in services like Lovable and Bolt, or even in AGENTS.md. With local applications or local apps, security does not really matter.

## Future

Personal software will only advance in the future. There will be fewer and fewer SaaS apps targeted at normal people, and people will create software themselves instead. I think this will be a great time, because we can focus on solving bigger problems. For example, I find "Patch the Planet" from Geoffrey Huntley really interesting. We can start solving the world's bigger problems. The determining factor of what we can build is our imagination. This does not mean that there will be a lot of good ideas in the future, but it's easier to find out if an idea is good or not. So I am excited for the future.
