# My Experience as an Application Developer Apprentice in the 3rd Year and Thoughts About AI

After my 9th year of school, which everyone in Switzerland has to do, I started an apprenticeship in application development. I was 15 years old when I started and 14 when I signed the contract. This is nothing unusual. Most young people in my country do this. For context on when this was: the newest model released by OpenAI was GPT-4o. So why do I say this? In this blog post I mainly want to write about my experience with AI during my apprenticeship, and I want to start at the beginning.

## What Is an Apprenticeship?

Here's some context on what I'm doing. I live in Switzerland, where it is very common to do an apprenticeship after 9 years of school. What you have to learn is highly regulated. You spend 1.5 to 2 days a week at school and 3 to 3.5 days working at a company. At my company, you spend the first year in a bootcamp. In the second year, you go to one team for 6 months and then switch to another team. You have no say in which teams you're placed in during the second year. In the third and fourth years, you have to apply for an "internship." You then have an interview with the team, and they choose one candidate. After 4 years you get an EFZ, a federal certificate showing that you know application development. This is how it works in Switzerland.

## 1st Year

The first year was basically a bootcamp where we learned the basics of development. We started with hardware and learned on a basic level how a computer works. We learned some Python, Linux, SQL, Java, Spring Boot, React for web development, Agile, and what the cloud is and how to use Azure. So, just the basics. Of course, I wasn't a pro at everything (or even at one thing), but it was very good for introducing us to the world of software engineering. That year, AI wasn't forbidden, but it was discouraged because you're supposed to learn something. It also mattered that you actually knew your stuff, because we had one-on-one technical interviews with our coaches, so they could tell very quickly if your project was written with AI. I mostly used it to ask questions, like Google. AI was also really limited at the time, but our problems weren't that difficult either. The most important thing about the first year was that AI was more of a gimmick than a really useful tool. So I didn't think much about whether it could take my future job, but that would soon change.

## 2nd Year

In the second year, we were put in a real production team inside our company or its sister company. This was a big step. I learned a lot, and I'm really grateful for the opportunity to see how software is made in the real world. My mentor was also really nice. He almost always took time when I had questions, even when they weren't directly related to my task. I soon started getting tickets on the real application, not some side project nobody uses. I really appreciated that, because some of my friends in other teams didn't get "real-world work." I also learned that inside a team, not everything works as planned or follows the practices we learned in the first year. I still sometimes think it's a wonder that the application is still running. So I learned a lot. In the second half of the year, I was back at the bootcamp, writing and maintaining internal tools and helping the first-year apprentices with simple questions. I didn't learn as much there because it isn't a production team, but I had more freedom to do things my way, and I had a great time with my coaches.

### The Release of Opus 4.5

On November 24, 2025, Anthropic released Claude Opus 4.5, which many consider the first model that could really do tasks on its own. At the time, I followed AI news through YouTubers like ThePrimeagen and Theo. That was the first time I thought AI could do a reasonable job at simple tasks. I still heard a lot of voices saying AI can't do much, but I also thought that all the people who embrace AI and find it useful can't all be wrong.

## 3rd Year

As I'm writing this, I'm in the third year of my apprenticeship. I'm on a new team where I'll stay for at least a year, maybe for the rest of my apprenticeship. My new team is great, especially my new mentor. He really knows his stuff. Often when we talk about my tasks from the week, we drift into something technical, and sometimes he shows me how something in the application works. I really appreciate working with him. I also like the tasks I'm getting. They're interesting, and I get to implement new specs. The hardest part of my job right now isn't the coding. It's understanding the system and the business processes behind it.

## AI in My Apprenticeship

AI is generally discouraged in my apprenticeship because the main goal isn't productivity. Heck, I get paid to learn. With AI, the risk of outsourcing your learning is big. Still, I used it to answer questions and help me solve difficult problems. I didn't use agents at work because they weren't allowed and I wouldn't have learned anything. Now, in my third year, any agent other than GitHub Copilot is forbidden. You have to order a license through the internal IT shop, and licenses are limited to 150 people. There are 600 devs in the company (you see the problem?), so I didn't get one. I can generally still use any AI web chat, but that will also be banned soon because of data privacy. The company fears that CID (Customer Identifying Data) could leak through AI web interfaces. After that, only the Copilot integrated into Teams will be allowed.

## The Fear of AI Replacing Me

Like I already wrote, the idea of AI replacing me started with the release of Opus 4.5, but it was more of a hypothetical scenario in my head than a real fear. Then models kept improving. I tried them myself, saw what they can do, and saw that they're useful. I also read articles saying that hiring of junior engineers has gone down. So there's this fear that after these 4 years (1.75 years left), I'll have learned something nobody will hire me for. The job market in my country doesn't look good, and even my own company is outsourcing work to Portugal and cutting jobs. Before my apprenticeship, I was told that around 90% of apprentices get a full-time offer afterward, but I'm not sure I will. Lots of software engineers in my country have been laid off, so who will choose a junior engineer? Alongside my apprenticeship, I'm doing the BM (Berufsmatura in German). This means a bit more school, but afterward I can do a bachelor's degree. That still gives me hope that I can do something else if this doesn't work out.

## What to Do Now

After explaining my fear, the only solution I've come up with is to be as good as possible at my job, learn as much as possible, and keep up with AI. I plan to do this by reading blogs like Simon Willison's and occasionally checking Hacker News. But at the end of the day, I have to realize that I can't change what's happening with AI. I can only change how I act.
